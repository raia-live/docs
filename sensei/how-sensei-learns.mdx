Sensei improves from one source: how your work turned out. This page defines the terms the dashboard uses and follows one task through the loop.

## The terms

A **session** is the calls that belong to one task. A **turn** is one ask from you and every model call Sensei made before it answered.

An **outcome** says how a turn went. It is one of `success`, `minor_failure`, `failure` or `critical_failure`. An outcome is **verified** when a person, an editor hook, CI or a harness reported it. When nobody reports one, Sensei reads the outcome itself from the checks that ran in the turn. The dashboard labels that case **Sensei's read**.

**Learnings** are what Sensei keeps from finished sessions: a summary of each turn, a map of the codebase, and what you told it to do differently. Later sessions on the same project start with the Learnings that fit the ask.

A **project** groups API keys. Sessions on those keys belong to the project, and so do its Learnings and its model.

A **version** is a model trained on the turns of one project that ended in a verified success. Versions are named `v1`, `v2` and so on. Before any version exists, a project's keys are served the **included model**.

## One task through the loop

1. You ask for a change in your editor. The calls are grouped into a session.
2. The turn ends. Your tests passed, and the editor hook posts `success`. Without a hook, Sensei reads the result from the conversation.
3. The outcome seals the turn. Sensei writes a summary of what was asked, which files were read and edited, which commands ran and how it ended.
4. The next session on that project asks for something related. Sensei puts the earlier summary in front of the model before it answers.
5. On Team, the turn is also kept for 30 days as training material for the project's next version. See [Your model](/sensei/your-model).

## The learning loop on Overview

The **Learning loop** card on **Overview** counts the last 30 days at five points. A weak point shows a warning under its ring.

| Link | What it counts |
| --- | --- |
| Sealed | Sessions in which a turn got an outcome. A session with no outcome taught nothing. |
| Verified | Turns whose outcome came from a person, a hook, CI or a harness. The rest carry Sensei's read, or went idle before an answer and are left out. |
| Agreed | Turns that have both a verified outcome and Sensei's read, and where the two say the same. This is how far Sensei's read can be trusted when it stands alone. |
| Recorded | Outcomes and session notes that reached your Learnings. Writes are queued and retried. One that failed after ten attempts shows a **Retry** button. |
| Reused | Sessions that started with an earlier session on at least half the same ask. |

Under the rings, the card compares turns that built on an earlier session with turns that did not, and gives the success rate of each group. It is a count of what happened. While the groups are small the card shows an **Early** badge.

## What raises the quality of what Sensei learns

A posted result stands above Sensei's read. Two things produce verified outcomes with little effort:

- The [editor hook](/sensei/outcomes#from-your-editor) posts the verdict of the last check when a session ends.
- [One request from CI](/sensei/outcomes#from-ci-or-a-script) marks a session after your pipeline finishes.

For runs, marking a run **Right** or **Wrong** on its page does the same job. See [Review a run](/sensei/runs/review#say-how-it-turned-out).
